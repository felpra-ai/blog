## What this is

- A weekly **volume-surge screen**: traded names whose recent trading volume ran well above their own prior average (data as of 2026-11-20).
- Screened **467** names; **15** qualified — the top ones are tabled below.
- **Returning names**: Palo Alto Networks, Inc.(PANW) (20w ago, since -2.4%), Williams-Sonoma, Inc.(WSM) (12w ago, since -11.7%), Everpure, Inc.(P) (8w ago, since -14.5%), Veeva Systems Inc.(VEEV) (12w ago, since -5.7%) — flagged vs prior appearances.
- Surging volume signals **attention**, not direction — it accompanies both rallies and selloffs. This is an observational screen, **not investment advice**.

## What drew volume

Attention this week did not settle on any one theme. Of 467 names screened, 15 cleared the surge bar, and they came from all over the market: a utility, a handful of software names, a retailer, a copper miner. The loudest by far was AES, trading at 6.9 times its usual volume while its price barely moved. Palo Alto and Everpure kept technology on the list, and Target turned over heavily and ended the window almost exactly where it started.

## Reading the tape

Look past the individual days and the clearer thread is how much of this volume came with selling. UnitedHealth lost 14.5% on heavy trade. Intuit fell 20.0%, and Freeport-McMoRan gave up 26.2%, the steepest drop on the list. Williams-Sonoma and Veeva also gave back double digits. These are large companies in healthcare, software, materials and home furnishings, so the selling was not confined to one corner, and the weight of the week sits on that side. A few names rose: Palo Alto, Expeditors and Take-Two each edged higher, though none by much.

Heavy volume records where attention went.
